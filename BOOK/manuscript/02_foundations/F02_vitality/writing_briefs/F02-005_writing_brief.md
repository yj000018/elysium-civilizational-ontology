# F02-005 — Regeneration: The Civilizational Imperative

## Purpose
Establish regeneration not as an environmental aspiration but as the civilizational imperative — the active restoration of living systems as a condition of civilizational survival and the final synthesis of Foundation 02.

## Must include
1. Regeneration as paradigm shift: from extraction and conservation to active restoration.
2. The three scales of regeneration: individual (lifestyle, diet, microbiome), societal (agroecology, rewilding, urban greening), civilizational (economic redesign, governance for living systems).
3. Regenerative agriculture as proof of concept: soil carbon sequestration, biodiversity recovery, yield resilience.
4. The economic case for regeneration: natural capital accounting, true cost economics.
5. The governance challenge: who owns the right to regenerate? Land rights, indigenous knowledge, commons.
6. The civilizational vision: a civilization that actively heals the living systems it depends on.
7. Synthesis of F02: Health, Food, Biodiversity, Ecosystems, Regeneration as an integrated vital system.
8. Bridge to Foundation 03 (Agency).

## Hard constraints
- Do not become utopian without grounding in existing practice.
- Do not reduce to carbon sequestration metrics.
- Do not ignore the political economy of land and resource ownership.
- This is the closing module of F02 — it must synthesize and close, not open new threads.

## Key idea
Regeneration is not the opposite of extraction. It is the next civilizational paradigm — one in which human activity becomes a net positive force for the living systems it depends on.

## Suggested closing direction
"Vitality is not a given. It is a choice — and a practice. The question that follows is therefore: who has the agency to make that choice? Foundation 03 begins."
