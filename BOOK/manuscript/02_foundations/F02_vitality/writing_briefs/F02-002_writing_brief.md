# F02-002 — Food & Agriculture: The Metabolic Foundation

## Purpose
Establish food and agriculture as the metabolic interface between civilization and living systems — the point where human society either regenerates or degrades the biological substrate.

## Must include
1. Agriculture as civilization's first metabolic contract with living systems.
2. The industrial food system: simplification, monoculture, chemical dependency, soil degradation.
3. Food as health determinant — the direct link between food systems and civilizational health (F02-001).
4. The global food system's structural fragility: supply chain concentration, seed monopolies, price volatility.
5. The pathology of ultra-processed food as civilizational symptom.
6. The regenerative direction: agroecology, food sovereignty, soil as living system.
7. Bridge to Biodiversity.

## Hard constraints
- Do not become an agricultural policy paper.
- Do not reduce to organic vs. conventional framing.
- Do not ignore the political economy of food.

## Key idea
Food systems are not a supply chain problem. They are the metabolic relationship between civilization and the living world — and the health of that relationship determines the health of everything else.

## Suggested closing direction
"But food systems do not exist in isolation. They depend on something older and more fundamental: the diversity of life itself."
