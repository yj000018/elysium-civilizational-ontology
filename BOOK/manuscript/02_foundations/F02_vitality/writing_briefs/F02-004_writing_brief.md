# F02-004 — Ecosystems: The Living Architecture

## Purpose
Establish ecosystems as the living architecture that mediates between civilization and the biosphere — the organized, self-regulating systems that provide the services upon which all civilizational activity depends.

## Must include
1. Ecosystems as self-organizing living systems — not collections of species but functional architectures.
2. Ecosystem services: provisioning, regulating, cultural, supporting — and their economic invisibility.
3. Tipping points and regime shifts: the non-linear collapse dynamics of ecosystems.
4. The planetary boundaries framework: which systems are approaching or have crossed thresholds.
5. The pathology of externalization: treating ecosystem services as free and infinite.
6. The link between ecosystem degradation and civilizational instability (climate, water, food, conflict).
7. The regenerative direction: ecosystem-based management, nature-based solutions, payment for ecosystem services.
8. Bridge to Regeneration.

## Hard constraints
- Do not reduce to climate change framing alone.
- Do not become an ecology textbook.
- Do not ignore the governance and economic dimensions of ecosystem collapse.

## Key idea
Ecosystems are not background scenery. They are the living architecture of the biosphere — and their degradation is the degradation of civilization's operating conditions.

## Suggested closing direction
"But ecosystems do not only need protection. They need active restoration. The final question of Vitality is therefore the most ambitious: can civilization become a regenerative force?"
